How the reports were sorted

  1. One report, one count. The export repeats some reports across its pages. Each id is counted once.
  2. Read the body, not the subject. Most bodies name two dates: what was booked and what arrived. In almost all of them the second is the first with day and month swapped. A swap is a flip bug.
  3. Flip bugs split by tenant and channel, not by intake tag. Every tenant on file writes the day first. A report through the form of a booking-v3 tenant is hint: that form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Everything else is parser. The intake tag is a guess from the browser and is often wrong.
  4. A one-day move is not a swap. The reports the desk already marked as a one-day move (desk notes) describe a date that moves by a single day: the weekday is off by one, a client in Lisbon and a client in Toronto see different days, an invite arrives before the booking was made. A month-first parser cannot do that. The desk files them under parser by tenant and footnotes them; they stay counted there, and here they are also a problem of their own.
